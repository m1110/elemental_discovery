# From deep thought to no thought: how modes of mind shape lived time

*A research deep dive plus a computational model (`depthtime/`).*

---

## 0. The question in one paragraph

Clock time passes at one fixed rate, but lived time does not. An hour of step-by-step problem
solving vanishes. An hour in a waiting room drags. A 30-minute sit can feel like 45 minutes to a
mindful beginner and like 5 minutes to a long-term practitioner who "dropped out of time".
A two-minute BJJ scramble feels like a few seconds while it happens, but you can replay it
in detail for days. This document asks **what each mode of thinking does to the machinery that
produces felt time, and why the effect is not a straight line from "deep thought" to "no thought."**
The short answer: **as thought quiets, time first speeds up, then slows down, then disappears.**
These three changes come from three different mechanisms.

---

## 1. What "time perception" actually means: four separate readouts

A recurring source of confusion, in the popular literature and in parts of the scientific
literature, is treating "time perception" as one thing. Researchers separate at least four
readouts, and the practices discussed here move them in *different directions*:

| Readout | Question it answers | Typical measure |
|---|---|---|
| **Prospective duration** | "How long is this taking?" while you know you will be asked | Interval reproduction or production, verbal estimate with forewarning |
| **Passage of time (PoT)** | "How fast does time seem to be going?" | Likert "slow ... fast" |
| **Retrospective duration** | "How long did that last?" asked afterwards, without forewarning | Verbal estimate after a surprise question |
| **Temporal presence / timelessness** | "Was there a sense of time at all?" | Phenomenological questionnaires, e.g. the timelessness and selflessness items in the work of Berkovich-Ohana, Wittmann and colleagues |

Classic dissociation: Block & Zakay's meta-analyses (1997; Block, Hancock & Zakay 2010)
show that **cognitive load makes prospective estimates *shorter* but retrospective estimates
*longer*.** So a practice can make time "fly" *and* "feel long" at once. That is the holiday
paradox described by Claudia Hammond.

## 2. The mechanisms (what the model is built from)

### 2.1 The internal clock: pacemaker, gate, accumulator
*Treisman 1963; Gibbon 1977 (Scalar Expectancy Theory); Zakay & Block 1997 (Attentional Gate Model).*

- A **pacemaker** emits pulses. Its rate rises with **arousal** (Droit-Volet & Meck 2007 review
  emotional time distortions). Pharmacological and clinical work links clock speed to
  dopamine (Meck; Matell & Meck 2004, striatal beat-frequency model).
- An **attentional gate** lets pulses into the accumulator only when attention is on time.
  Anything that captures attention, such as reasoning, a hard physical task or vivid mind-wandering,
  **closes the gate**, so fewer pulses are counted and **time flies**.
- Timing variability scales with duration (**scalar property**, Weber fraction ~0.1–0.25).

### 2.2 The body as clock: interoception and the insula
*Craig 2009 ("Emotional moments across time"); Wittmann 2009, 2013 (*Felt Time*); Meissner & Wittmann 2011.*

Craig and Wittmann propose that the felt present is built from a stream of interoceptive
"global emotional moments" integrated in the posterior-to-anterior insula. Felt duration then
partly reflects **how much bodily self-state accumulates**. Meissner & Wittmann (2011) found that
cardiac interoceptive signals relate to duration-reproduction accuracy. This is the main bridge
to body-based practices: **training interoceptive attention should speed the "body clock" and
make the present feel fuller.**

### 2.3 Memory: time remembered is change remembered
*Ornstein 1969 (storage-size model); Block & Zakay 1997; Zacks et al. (event segmentation);
Roseboom et al. 2019, Nature Communications (duration estimated from accumulated salient change in
perceptual-classification networks); Fountas et al. 2022.*

Looking back, we estimate duration from **how many distinct events or context changes were
stored**. New places, shifting thoughts and emotional peaks add boundaries. Monotony and blankness
add none. Stetson, Fiesta & Eagleman (2007) showed that people falling in a free-fall drop
*remember* the fall as longer but did not gain moment-to-moment temporal resolution. That
supports a **memory-density account of "slow motion" under threat**.

### 2.4 The self that reads the clock
*Wittmann 2015 (Consciousness & Cognition, "Modulations of the experience of self and time");
Berkovich-Ohana et al. 2011, 2013 (mindfulness-trained brains; timelessness and selflessness
states); Dor-Ziderman et al. 2013.*

Time is felt **by someone**. The temporal self has three overlapping supports:
1. **Conceptual / narrative content**: thinking *in* time (plans, memories, the next reasoning step).
2. **Self-referential processing**: "me", "my performance", "how long have I been here?"
   (default-mode network hubs: medial prefrontal and posterior cingulate cortex).
3. **Bodily self-awareness**: the interoceptive self of 2.2.

Expert meditators who voluntarily enter states of reduced self-boundary report **timelessness
together with selflessness**. Berkovich-Ohana's MEG/EEG work found that these reports co-occur
with changes in self-referential networks. When all three supports drop away together,
duration is no longer *represented*. This differs from "time flies". It is *time is absent*.

## 3. The modes of thinking, one by one

> Evidence grades: **A** = multiple controlled studies; **B** = some direct studies, mixed or small;
> **C** = indirect: mechanism-based inference from related findings; little or no direct
> time-perception research on this practice.

### 3.1 Sequential, deliberate thinking (deep problem solving) — *A*
- Step-by-step reasoning is high **cognitive load** with focused attention. Prospective
  estimates shrink and the passage of time speeds up (Block, Hancock & Zakay 2010).
- Each reasoning step is a new context, so **retrospective duration grows**. A long session of
  hard thinking "flew" but afterwards feels like it filled a big block of the day.
- Self-reference is *low*, because attention is on the problem, but conceptual content is maximal,
  so the temporal self stays intact. **No timelessness.**

### 3.2 Authentic introspection (reflective self-inquiry, journaling) — *B/C*
- Attention turns on one's own states. Self-reference and interoceptive awareness rise, and
  discursive content stays moderate.
- Wittmann's framework predicts that **heightened self-awareness lengthens felt duration**:
  more self-state accumulates per unit time. Rumination and boredom slow time (Watt 1991;
  boredom studies by Zakay 2014 and Wittmann's group). Experience-sampling work also links
  self-focused negative affect to slower passage.
- Net effect: introspection sits near ordinary time, slightly slower than sequential thinking,
  with **rich retrospective memory** (insights are events).
- Important caveat: "authentic" introspection, meaning curious, non-ruminative and accepting, is
  close to open-monitoring meditation. *Ruminative* introspection behaves like anxious waiting.

### 3.3 Meditation — *A for short mindfulness inductions, B for expert states*
**Focused attention (breath, mantra) and open monitoring (vipassana / noting)**
- Brief mindfulness inductions in novices lead to **overestimation of durations** and a sense of
  **time slowing** (Kramer, Weger & Sharma 2013; Droit-Volet, Fanget & Dambrun 2015;
  Droit-Volet et al. 2018–2019). Effects are modest and not always replicated at short intervals.
- Experienced meditators report that recent weeks and months felt *longer*, an expansion of
  extended time-spans (Wittmann et al. 2015, Frontiers in Psychology). They also show better
  duration-discrimination in some paradigms (Wittmann & Schmidt 2014 review; Berkovich-Ohana 2011).
- Mechanism: present-moment and interoceptive attention **opens the gate** and **feeds the body
  clock**. Fine-grained noticing, "rising ... passing ... itching ...", creates many small
  events for memory.

**Concentrative absorption (jhāna-like states) and nondual / objectless resting**
- Practitioner reports across traditions describe states without inner speech and sometimes
  without a felt "doer", accompanied by **timelessness**. Lab work on such states is limited and
  relies on small samples of adepts (Berkovich-Ohana 2013; Hagerty et al. 2013 single-subject
  jhāna study; Ataria et al. on boundary dissolution). *Evidence B/C.*
- A common report: a 45-minute sit "lasted five minutes". After-the-fact duration shrinks,
  because nothing was encoded *as an event for a self*. During the state there is **no felt
  passage at all**, which is not the same as fast passage.

### 3.4 Yoga — *B*
- A class moves through stages. **Asana** is breath-linked movement with high interoceptive load.
  **Pranayama** is breath regulation that shifts autonomic arousal. **Savasana / yoga nidra** is
  deep rest that sits between absorption and sleep onset.
- Thönes & Wittmann (2016) examined yogic meditation practitioners and reported effects on
  retrospective duration and passage-of-time judgments consistent with the mindfulness pattern.
- Model expectation: asana is slightly slow and full, pranayama is slow, and savasana either
  becomes timeless for experienced practitioners or becomes **dozing**. Sleep is the everyday
  model of timelessness, with retrospective duration lost.

### 3.5 Tai chi — *C*
- Slow, continuous, whole-body movement under sustained attention: "meditation in motion".
  Tai chi training improves attention, balance and interoceptive/proprioceptive awareness
  (various RCTs in older adults). Direct time-perception studies are scarce.
- Prediction: unlike fast sports, the motor demand is low enough that **present-moment
  awareness dominates the gate**. Experts should feel *more* time (slow, spacious), not less.
  This is the clearest case where *movement does not make time fly*.

### 3.6 Qigong / zhan zhuang (standing post) — *C*
- Mostly still postures with breath and interoceptive ("qi") attention. Phenomenologically
  close to body-scan meditation, plus mild muscular effort and arousal.
- Prediction: like open monitoring, with a strong interoceptive clock. Long standing practice can
  also tip into absorption with reduced retrospective duration.

### 3.7 Brazilian jiu-jitsu (rolling) — *C (mechanisms A)*
- A live, resisting opponent; chess-like **sequential problem solving under threat** of chokes
  and joint locks; very high motor demand. Attention is fully captured, so **rounds fly by**.
- Threat spikes, such as a near-submission, a scramble or an escape, combine **high arousal**,
  which speeds the clock and strengthens encoding, with **action preparation**. Hagura et al.
  (2012, Proc. R. Soc. B) showed that preparing a rapid action slows subjective time and improves
  temporal resolution. Practitioners report "slow motion" moments inside fast rounds.
- Memory is dense (novelty and arousal), so **the session is remembered as long**. This is the
  holiday paradox in miniature.
- Expertise lowers threat reactivity, so experts feel *fewer* slow-motion moments and more
  smooth flow. Many describe "having all the time in the world" (chunking and anticipation:
  experts see positions as wholes and react earlier).

### 3.8 Parkour — *C (mechanisms A)*
- Novel terrain, real risk, and repeated **commitment moments** at the edge of a jump, which
  are the purest form of action preparation. Flow research (Csikszentmihalyi; Rutrecht et al.
  2021 found flow associated with time passing faster) predicts time flying during lines.
- Each jump is a strongly encoded event, so remembered duration is long.
- Prediction: the most "time-dilated *moments* inside time-compressed *sessions*" profile.

### 3.9 Flow in general
Flow, meaning the challenge–skill balance with action–awareness merging, is the hinge state of
the whole axis. It has **low self-reference and high absorption, with conceptual content still
present**, so time flies but does not vanish (Csikszentmihalyi's "transformation of time";
Rutrecht et al. 2021; Im & Varma 2018).

## 4. The synthesis: the depth axis is not monotonic

Arrange the modes by **how much discursive thought is released while attention stays gathered**:

```
 depth 0.00   deep sequential thought      content high, focus high, self low
 depth 0.25   absorbed task focus / flow   content mid, focus very high, self very low
 depth 0.50   present-moment awareness     content low, awareness very high, bare self present
 depth 0.75   concentrative absorption     content ~0, focus total, awareness narrowing
 depth 1.00   no-thought / cessation-like  nothing to read the clock
```

Ordinary mind-wandering is **off-axis**. It is the state every practice falls back into
(~47 % of waking time in Killingsworth & Gilbert's 2010 experience-sampling study).

**Central prediction (Figure `figures/depth_curve.png`):**

1. **Deep thought: time flies.** The gate is closed by content (prospective ≈ 0.7×).
   Memory is rich (≈ 1.5× retrospective).
2. **Present-moment awareness: time slows.** The gate opens and the body clock accelerates
   (prospective ≈ 1.4× at steady state; much smaller, ≈ 1.05–1.2×, in real novice sessions
   because of mind-wandering).
3. **No-thought: time disappears.** The temporal self-model collapses. Prospective and
   retrospective durations go toward zero, but the felt *passage* does not speed up. There is
   simply no passage.

So "fast", "slow" and "absent" are three different phenomena with three different causes.
They are not points on one speed dial.

## 5. The model (`depthtime`)

### 5.1 State variables (all in [0, 1])
`content`, `focus`, `awareness` (present-moment/interoceptive), `self_ref`, `arousal`,
`movement`, `novelty`, `action_prep`. The first four are set by depth (piecewise-linear between
the anchors above). The last four are set by the practice context.

### 5.2 Equations (relative to the ordinary-mind reference)

```
task demand    D = content·(0.45 + 0.55·focus)  ⊕  0.85·movement·(0.5 + 0.5·focus)     (⊕ = probabilistic OR)
gate           g = (1 − 0.55·D) · (1 + 0.15·awareness)
pacemaker      r = clock_rate · (1 + 0.8·(arousal − 0.3) + 0.08·interoception·awareness + 0.5·action_prep)
self-model     R = 1 − [(1−content)(1−self_ref)(1−awareness)]^1.5
event rate     n = novelty + 0.8·content + 0.45·awareness·(1−content) + 0.5·action_prep
encoding       m = R · (0.35 + 0.5·max(focus, awareness)) · (1 + 0.6·arousal)

prospective    = r·g·R / ref
passage        = ref / (r·g)                (only meaningful in proportion to R)
retrospective  = (n·m / ref)^0.6           (compressive memory-to-duration mapping)
timelessness   = 1 − R
```

### 5.3 The individual
`Individual` traits: `experience` (reachable depth = 0.45 + 0.55·experience, settling speed,
lower mind-wandering and threat reactivity), `interoception`, `mind_wandering`,
`arousal_reactivity`, `clock_rate`, `weber`. Archetypes: novice, intermediate, expert,
anxious novice.

### 5.4 Session dynamics
Second-by-second simulation. Depth relaxes toward the practice's target with a time constant
set by experience. Mind-wandering episodes form a Poisson process and knock depth back to the
entry level. Threat and jump spikes override context. Felt durations are integrated and
receive scalar (log-normal) timing noise. Monte Carlo over sessions.

### 5.6 Testable predictions
1. **Dissociation by expertise**: the *same* 30-min "objectless" instruction yields
   *lengthened* prospective time in novices but *shortened* retrospective time and high
   timelessness in adepts.
2. **Movement is not the variable; attentional demand is.** Tai chi and qigong should lengthen
   time like seated mindfulness. BJJ and parkour should compress it.
3. **Moments vs sessions** in combat and risk sports: in-the-moment dilation during threat or
   commitment spikes, but whole sessions compressed prospectively and expanded retrospectively.
4. **Interoceptive accuracy moderates** meditation-induced lengthening (heartbeat-detection
   score × condition interaction).
5. **Timelessness tracks the joint drop** of inner speech, self-reference and body awareness.
   The drop of any one alone should not produce it. This is measurable with experience sampling
   plus DMN and insula markers.
6. **Ruminative introspection behaves like waiting**: slow and remembered short. Curious,
   accepting introspection behaves like open monitoring.

### 5.7 Measuring yourself (n-of-1 protocol) and fitting the model
For each session, record: practice, clock minutes, an in-session estimate (ask a partner or
an app to interrupt at random: "how long so far?"), a surprise retrospective estimate, and a
−3…+3 passage rating. Hide clocks. Vary session lengths so you cannot learn the answer.
Then run:

```
python -m depthtime fit examples/my_sessions.csv
```

The fit returns your estimated experience, interoception, mind-wandering, arousal reactivity
and clock rate, and the depth the model thinks you can reach.

## 6. Limitations and honest caveats
- **Parameters are hypotheses, not estimates.** Defaults were tuned so the model reproduces the
  *direction* of established findings (unit tests in `tests/` encode each one). The magnitudes,
  especially for experts, are speculative.
- **Evidence is uneven.** Mindfulness and time has a real experimental literature. Tai chi,
  qigong, BJJ and parkour have almost no *direct* time-perception studies, so predictions there
  come from mechanisms (load, arousal, action preparation, interoception, novelty).
- **Emotion/valence is simplified.** Passage-of-time judgments depend strongly on affect and
  boredom (Droit-Volet & Wearden 2016). Here valence enters only through arousal and via the
  "waiting" control.
- **Sleep and hypoxia** (savasana dozing, chokes) produce genuine gaps in consciousness. The
  model represents them only indirectly through the self-model.
- **Reports of timelessness are retrospective**, and the "self that reports" is reconstructed.
  First-person methods (micro-phenomenological interviews) are essential complements.
- Citations are given as orientation for further reading. Verify specifics, such as exact effect
  sizes and paradigms, against the original papers before relying on them.
